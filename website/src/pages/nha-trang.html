Vietnam Guide: Nha Trang

Living in Nha Trang as an Expat

Everything you need to know about moving to Nha Trang.

Nha Trang is a well-established beach city on Vietnam’s south-central coast, built around a long curve of bay with islands offshore, one of the country’s best city beaches and warm, swimmable water for most of the year. It is cheap, sunny and relaxed, and it is Vietnam’s main hub for Russian-speaking residents. This guide covers where to live, renting and its traps, costs, the seasons, getting around, healthcare, schools and daily life.

Since July 2025, Nha Trang has been part of an enlarged Khanh Hoa province, which merged with Ninh Thuan, and Vietnam has removed the district level of government. Everyone still uses the familiar neighborhood names, and so do we.

Why Expats Choose Nha Trang

  • The beach: a long bay, a well-kept waterfront promenade and warm sea for most of the year
  • Cost: one of the cheaper places to live well in Vietnam, where a good dinner out barely dents the budget
  • Pace of life: sunny, low-stress and slower than the big cities
  • A ready-made community for Russian speakers: shops, cafés, schools, doctors and agents working in Russian
  • Diving and islands: clear water in the dry season and islands to explore in the bay
  • Access: Cam Ranh International Airport, south of the city, with flights across Vietnam and the region

It suits others less well. Digital nomads looking for a big, English-speaking scene usually prefer Da Nang, families wanting a wide choice of international schools will find few options, and parts of the city are very focused on tourism.

Best Neighborhoods for Expats in Nha Trang

Almost everything that matters to expats sits on or just behind the bay. The beach boulevard, Tran Phu, runs about 7 km along the sea, lined with hotels, seafood restaurants and bars.

The center

The central area between Tran Phu, Nguyen Thien Thuat and Hung Vuong is where tourists and short-term visitors cluster. Nguyen Thien Thuat is the main eating street, with Vietnamese, Korean, Japanese and Russian restaurants and mini-markets, and Hung Vuong handles shopping and errands. Everything is walkable, but it is the most expensive and noisiest part of town, and unmistakably touristy.

The north: Vinh Hai and Vinh Phuoc

Long-term residents, especially Russian speakers, tend to move north. It is quieter, cleaner and noticeably cheaper than the center, with beaches that empty out quickly. The large Muong Thanh Oceanus towers, close to the sand, are the heart of the Russian-speaking community, with shops, cafés and services in Russian.

The west

Around Big C and Vinh Diem Trung, the west trades sea views for newer, gated apartment buildings, at lower prices, about 10 to 15 minutes by scooter from the beach. It is low-lying, though, and floods first in heavy rain, so check a building’s flood history before you sign.

The south and An Vien

The south, near Lotte Mart, is greener and calmer, with fewer restaurants. Further south, An Vien is a gated villa enclave with private-beach calm and an international school, popular with affluent families.

Sea-view condos

Many people move to Nha Trang for a sea view. Ocean-facing apartments with a pool, in towers such as Muong Thanh Oceanus, Scenia Bay and Gold Coast, cost clearly more than similar flats facing the city, and condition and appliances drive the price more than the number of rooms.

Renting in Nha Trang

Rents in Nha Trang are very negotiable. Landlords will often come down 10–20% for a multi-month commitment, and prices soften in the wet low season from about September. The north and west are typically 20–30% cheaper than the center, and sea views carry a clear premium. Our Cost of Living Calculator uses a planning estimate of around $420 a month for a mid-range one-bedroom apartment. Budget separately for management and security fees, water, internet and electricity.

Avoid the common traps

  • Misleading photos: always view in person, or on a live video call, before paying anything
  • Deposits that don’t come back: get everything in writing, photograph the apartment’s condition when you move in, and don’t pay more upfront than you need to
  • Electricity overcharging: many landlords charge a flat VND 3,500–4,000 per kWh, well above the official tariff. Ask the rate before you sign and check the meter yourself.
  • Prepaid chat listings: avoid paying in advance for places found through messaging groups, and use an established platform or a recommended agent

Landlords usually ask for a deposit of one or two months’ rent, plus rent paid in advance. Make sure your landlord registers your temporary residence with the local police, which is required for every foreigner.

Cost of Living in Nha Trang

Nha Trang is one of the more affordable places to live well in Vietnam, and cheaper than Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City. Rent is the biggest cost, and it varies a lot with the season, the floor, the view and the furnishing. Food is excellent value: phở, bánh mì, fresh seafood and Russian cafés are all cheap and everywhere, and a relaxed dinner for two costs very little.

Keep in mind

  • Electricity is where bills rise, especially with air conditioning through the hot summer
  • Monthly scooter rental is cheap, and Grab motorbike rides are everywhere
  • A solid home internet line is inexpensive
  • Imported goods cost more, though the Russian grocery stores stock many European staples

Seasons and Weather in Nha Trang

The weather is the single biggest thing newcomers underestimate here.

  • February to April: the best time of year, with warm days, calm water and diving visibility that can reach 20 meters
  • May to August: still dry, but very hot and humid, with a fierce sun
  • September to December: the wet season. October and November are unpredictable: some years bring short evening showers, others bring days of heavy rain, rough seas and cancelled island trips
  • January: the northeast monsoon can bring wind and rough surf to the central beach, with red warning flags

Heavy rain can cause serious flooding. In November 2025, western Nha Trang flooded badly after extreme rainfall, with water spreading far inland. The low-lying western areas flood first, so factor that in if you rent inland to save money. If your life revolves around the beach, plan around February to August and treat October and November as indoor months.

Getting Around Nha Trang

Most people get around by scooter, and monthly rental is cheap. Grab motorbike and car rides are everywhere, and the bus network is limited. Traffic is chaotic and pedestrian crossings are often ignored, so cross by walking slowly and steadily and letting the traffic flow around you.

Vietnam requires a licence for any motorbike of 50cc or more. Residents need a Vietnamese licence, converted from their home licence, and riding without the right licence can invalidate your insurance. Helmets are compulsory and the drink-driving limit is zero.

Cam Ranh International Airport is south of the city, with domestic flights and international routes, many of them to Russia, Korea and China.

Working in Nha Trang

Nha Trang’s economy runs on tourism and hospitality, so most local jobs for foreigners are in hotels, resorts, diving, tourism and English teaching. Most expats here are remote workers, retirees or people with their own business. To work for a Vietnamese employer you need a work permit, which your employer applies for, and remote workers still need a visa that matches how long they stay and what they do.

Healthcare and Safety in Nha Trang

Healthcare

Vinmec International Hospital, on Tran Phu, is the main choice for expats, and the community keeps informal lists of trusted doctors. Serious or specialist cases often mean a trip to Ho Chi Minh City, so choose health insurance with evacuation cover. Pharmacies are everywhere, some with Russian-speaking staff, but don’t take antibiotics or strong medicines without a doctor’s advice.

Emergencies

Dial 115 for an ambulance, 113 for police and 114 for fire. Keep your insurance details and a copy of your passport with you.

Staying safe

Nha Trang is not dangerous. The main risks are rental scams (see above), the roads and the sea: swim where lifeguards are on duty and stay out of the water when red flags are up. Drink bottled or filtered water, not tap water.

Schools in Nha Trang

Nha Trang is workable for families, but the choice of schools is much smaller than in Da Nang, Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City. Options include the Singapore International School, the Cambridge-curriculum AVE Academy in An Vien, other bilingual schools, and The First Academy, which teaches the Russian federal curriculum with Russian teachers. The limited range of international schools is the most common reason internationally minded families choose another city, so visit and check places before you commit.

Daily Life and Community

The Russian-speaking community

Nha Trang has the largest Russian-speaking community in Vietnam, with Russian signs across the tourist streets and the north, Russian bakeries and grocery stores, Russian-run schools and Russian-speaking agents and doctors. Newcomers can rent, see a doctor and enroll their children without speaking Vietnamese or English, and the community is quick to warn each other about bad landlords and agents. The flip side is that it is easy to live here for years in a bubble and never really meet Vietnam.

Food and the outdoors

Vietnamese food here is superb, with excellent seafood, and not aggressively spicy unless you ask. Swim, dive and snorkel around the islands in the bay, and take a day out at the Vinpearl resort and theme park on Hon Tre island, which is an attraction, not a place to live.

The honest annoyances

Nha Trang is a working Vietnamese city, not a resort. Expect litter on some streets, drains after rain, late-night karaoke from neighbors, even in good buildings, and insects on lower floors. Building a wide social circle outside the Russian-speaking community takes effort. None of it is a dealbreaker, but it is the reality behind the beach photos.

Learn a few words of Vietnamese, such as xin chào (hello) and cảm ơn (thank you). It makes daily life easier and earns a lot of goodwill.

Weekend Escapes from Nha Trang

The islands of the bay are the obvious escape, for diving, snorkeling and beaches. Along the coast, quieter bays and beaches north and south of the city make easy day trips, and the cool highland city of Da Lat is a few hours inland by road. Cam Ranh airport connects you to Ho Chi Minh City, Hanoi, Da Nang and beyond.

Final Thoughts on Living in Nha Trang

Nha Trang suits three groups especially well: Russian speakers, who get a ready-made community; beach lovers, who get one of Vietnam’s best city beaches; and retirees and remote workers looking for a cheap, sunny, low-stress life.

The smartest way in is a trial month in the dry season, a short-term rental first, and a longer lease only once you know where you want to live. Plan for the wet season, protect yourself from rental traps, and get good health insurance and the right visa.

Frequently Asked Questions: Living in Nha Trang

Where do expats live in Nha Trang?

Short-term visitors tend to stay in the center, near Tran Phu and Nguyen Thien Thuat. Long-term residents often move north to Vinh Hai and Vinh Phuoc, which are quieter and cheaper, while the west offers newer apartments at lower prices and An Vien suits families wanting a villa.

Why are there so many Russians in Nha Trang?

Nha Trang has long been the most popular Vietnamese destination for Russian-speaking visitors, and a large community has settled here, with its own shops, schools and services.

When is the best time to move to Nha Trang?

February to April, in the dry season, when the weather is warm and the sea is calm. October and November are the most unpredictable months, with the risk of heavy rain and flooding.

Is Nha Trang good for digital nomads?

It works well for quiet remote workers who want a cheap, sunny base. Those looking for a big, English-speaking nomad community and more coworking spaces usually prefer Da Nang.

Is Nha Trang good for families?

It can be, but there are few international schools compared with Da Nang, Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City, so check school places before you commit.

What are the most common rental scams?

Misleading photos, deposits that are not returned and electricity charged well above the official rate. View in person, get everything in writing, photograph the apartment at move-in and agree the electricity rate before you sign.
