Vietnam Guide: Cost of Living

Cost of Living

Everything you need to know about moving to Vietnam.

The cost of living in Vietnam is low by Western standards, but it varies a lot by city and by lifestyle. Housing is the biggest expense, and central Ho Chi Minh City and the popular expat areas of Hanoi cost the most. Living locally is very cheap. Living like you did at home, with imported food and Western brands, costs a lot more.

Cost of living overview in Vietnam

Costs in Vietnam vary significantly by city:

  • Ho Chi Minh City is the most expensive, particularly District 1, Thao Dien and Thu Thiem
  • Hanoi is slightly cheaper overall, with lower rents outside Tay Ho and the old quarter
  • Da Nang offers beach living for noticeably less than the two big cities
  • Smaller cities such as Hoi An, Nha Trang and Da Lat offer even better value

These differences depend heavily on rent, which is typically the largest expense.

Key Cost Considerations for Expats in Vietnam

Your personal budget will depend on a few core categories. Understanding these upfront makes it much easier to plan realistically.

Housing and rent

Housing is the single biggest cost for most expats. Prices for modern apartments with a pool and gym are much higher than for local housing, and asking prices in expat areas keep rising. Typical monthly rents for a one-bedroom apartment:

  • Ho Chi Minh City: $600–$1,500 in Thao Dien, District 1 or Binh Thanh, and more in premium buildings
  • Hanoi: $700–$1,300 in Tay Ho (West Lake), or $500–$900 in Cau Giay
  • Da Nang: $380–$550 near An Thuong and My Khe beach

Landlords usually ask for a deposit of one or two months and rent paid one to three months in advance. Many buildings also charge a monthly management fee. Rents are negotiable, especially on longer leases.

Utilities and internet

Utilities are cheap, except for electricity if you run air conditioning all day.

  • Electricity: $40–$60 a month in cooler months, and $60–$100+ in the hot season with regular air conditioning
  • Water: usually under $10 a month
  • Fiber internet: around VND 190,000–310,000 ($7–$12) a month
  • Mobile plans with data: around VND 60,000–200,000 ($2–$8) a month

Many landlords charge a flat rate for electricity of around VND 3,500–4,000 per kWh, higher than the official EVN tariff, so check your lease.

Transportation

Getting around is cheap, and most expats use ride-hailing apps rather than owning a car.

  • Grab motorbike: usually VND 15,000–35,000 ($0.60–$1.40) for a short ride
  • Grab car: usually VND 90,000–140,000 ($3.50–$5.50) for a short ride in the city
  • Ho Chi Minh City Metro Line 1: VND 6,000–20,000 a trip, or VND 300,000 ($12) for a monthly pass
  • Motorbike rental: around $60–$80 a month on a long-term basis

Owning a car is expensive because of high import taxes and registration fees, and parking and traffic make it impractical in the big cities.

Food and dining

Food is one of the areas where Vietnam offers outstanding value, as long as you eat locally.

  • Groceries: around $120–$220 per person a month for local produce, and more if you buy imported products
  • Eating out: a street-food meal like phở or cơm tấm costs VND 40,000–60,000 ($1.50–$2.50), and a main course in a mid-range restaurant $7–$12
  • Coffee: VND 15,000–25,000 from a street stall, or VND 50,000–80,000 in a specialty café

Western restaurants, imported wine and cheese cost about the same as at home, or more.

Healthcare, insurance, and banking

Private healthcare in Vietnam is affordable, and the international hospitals in the big cities offer a good standard of care.

  • Private health insurance: often $50–$120 per person a month for a good regional plan, depending on age and cover
  • Doctor’s visit: around $60–$120 at an international hospital, and much less at local private hospitals
  • Bank accounts: many Vietnamese banks charge little or nothing a month for a standard account

Most expats rely on private health insurance. If you work for a Vietnamese employer, you will also pay into the public health insurance system through your salary.

Final Thoughts on Cost of Living in Vietnam

Vietnam is still one of the most affordable places in Asia to live well. Rents in the expat areas of Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi have risen, but food, transport, domestic help and healthcare remain far cheaper than in most Western countries.

The key trade-off is simple: the closer you live to how locals live, the cheaper Vietnam becomes. Imported food, international schools and premium apartments are where budgets grow quickly.

For many expats, the result is not just a lower cost of living, but a better quality of life for the same money.

Frequently Asked Questions: Cost of Living in Vietnam

Is Vietnam still affordable in 2026?

Yes. Rents in central Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi have risen, but day-to-day costs remain low, and cities such as Da Nang offer excellent value.

What is the biggest expense when living in Vietnam?

Housing is the largest cost for most expats, followed by private health insurance and, for families, international school fees.

How much do I need per month to live comfortably in Vietnam?

Many couples live comfortably on $2,000–$3,000 a month, depending on the city. Premium apartments, imported food and international schools can push that much higher.

Which is cheaper, Ho Chi Minh City or Hanoi?

Hanoi is generally a little cheaper, mainly because rents are lower. Everyday costs such as food and transport are similar in both cities.

Are groceries and eating out expensive in Vietnam?

No. Local food is very cheap, both in markets and in restaurants. Imported products and Western restaurants cost about the same as at home, or more.

Do I need a car in Vietnam?

No. Most expats use Grab, taxis and public transport, or ride a motorbike. Cars are expensive to buy and impractical in city traffic.

Are utilities expensive in Vietnam?

Water and internet are cheap. Electricity can be higher than expected in the hot season if you use air conditioning a lot, especially when the landlord charges a flat rate.

How much does private healthcare cost?

A visit to a doctor at an international hospital typically costs around $60–$120, and a good regional health insurance plan often costs $50–$120 per person a month.

How much do international schools cost?

Fees at international schools in Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi typically range from around $11,000 to $38,000 a year, depending on the school and grade.

Can I live cheaply on a fixed budget in Vietnam?

Yes, particularly outside the big cities. In Da Nang, Hoi An or Da Lat, a comfortable lifestyle can cost well under $2,000 a month for a couple.
