Vietnam Guide: Healthcare

Healthcare and Health Insurance

Everything you need to know about moving to Vietnam.

Healthcare in Vietnam is affordable, and in the big cities you’ll find modern private and international hospitals with English-speaking doctors. The public system is very cheap but crowded, and it mostly works in Vietnamese. Most expats rely on private care backed by good health insurance, and use public hospitals only for specific needs.

How Healthcare in Vietnam Works

Vietnam has a public healthcare system, run through a network of district, provincial and central hospitals, and a fast-growing private sector. The public system is funded through the national health insurance scheme, which covers most Vietnamese citizens and foreigners working here on a local contract.

In practice, most expats use the private system for almost everything. International hospitals and clinics in Ho Chi Minh City, Hanoi and Da Nang are where you’ll find English-speaking staff, shorter waits and familiar standards of care. Large public hospitals are still the national centres for some complex treatments, and many of their senior doctors also work in private clinics.

How to Access Public Healthcare in Vietnam

If you work for a Vietnamese employer on a contract of 12 months or more, you are generally required to join the national health insurance scheme. The contribution is 4.5% of salary: 3% paid by your employer and 1.5% by you. Your employer registers you with a primary healthcare facility, which is printed on your health insurance card.

Using your registered facility, or following a referral from it, gives you the full benefit, with insurance usually paying 80% of covered costs. Going elsewhere without a referral reduces or removes the cover, except in emergencies.

The Language Challenge: Public hospitals are busy, waiting times can be long, and staff often speak little English. Forms, signs and payments are in Vietnamese. If you use the public system, bring a Vietnamese speaker with you, especially for anything complex. Public cover also doesn’t pay for treatment at international hospitals, which is why most expats with a local contract still buy private insurance.

Private Healthcare in Vietnam

Private healthcare is widely available in the big cities, with international hospitals such as FV Hospital and Vinmec, international clinics such as Family Medical Practice, and a large number of local private hospitals. Many have English-speaking doctors, direct billing with major insurers and their own 24-hour emergency departments.

Private care is not free, but it is affordable compared to North America. A consultation at an international hospital typically costs around $60–$120, and less at a local private hospital. Tests, scans and hospital stays can add up quickly, so good insurance matters. Outside the big cities, private options are more limited, and serious cases are usually transferred to Ho Chi Minh City or Hanoi.

Public vs Private Healthcare in Vietnam: What’s Better?

It’s not really a question of one being better than the other. They serve different purposes.

The public system is very cheap and includes the country’s biggest specialist hospitals. But it is crowded, it runs in Vietnamese, and standards of comfort and privacy are well below what most expats expect.

The private system is fast, comfortable and set up for foreigners, with English-speaking staff and modern equipment. The trade-off is cost, which is why most expats pair it with private health insurance.

For most expats, private care is the default, with the public system as a back-up.

Health Insurance in Vietnam for Expats

Health insurance is the most important part of your healthcare plan in Vietnam. There are generally three options: Vietnamese insurance, regional Asia plans and international plans with worldwide cover.

For many expats living full-time in Vietnam, a local or regional plan (from insurers such as Bao Viet, PVI or Pacific Cross) is the most practical choice. It covers the main private and international hospitals, often with direct billing, at a lower price than worldwide cover. If you travel a lot or want the option of treatment in Singapore, Bangkok or at home, an international plan is worth the extra cost.

Check the Limits: Cheaper plans often cover inpatient care only, or set low annual and per-visit limits for outpatient care. Check the limits on outpatient visits, maternity, dental and medical evacuation before you buy, and whether the plan covers pre-existing conditions.

Premiums rise with age and depend on your medical history. A good regional plan often costs around $50–$120 per person per month for a healthy adult, while comprehensive worldwide cover can cost several times more, particularly after age 60.

What Insurance You Need for Your Vietnam Visa

Vietnam does not generally ask for proof of health insurance for an e-visa or a temporary residence card. That doesn’t mean you should go without it: foreigners pay for treatment themselves, and hospitals may ask for payment or a guarantee before treating you.

If you apply for a work permit, you’ll need a health certificate from an approved medical facility, issued within the last 12 months. Once you start work on a local contract of 12 months or more, you’ll usually join the national health insurance scheme through your employer, and most expats add private cover on top.

Pharmacies and Prescriptions in Vietnam

Pharmacies (nhà thuốc) are everywhere, and large chains such as Long Châu and Pharmacity have branches in every city. Many medicines that need a prescription in Western countries are sold over the counter in Vietnam, although the law requires prescriptions for antibiotics and other prescription-only medicines.

Foreign prescriptions are generally not accepted, so you’ll need a doctor in Vietnam to prescribe any regular medication. If you bring medication with you, keep it in its original packaging with your prescription, and check the rules first: controlled medicines, including some ADHD, sleep and pain medications, are strictly limited.

Final Thoughts on Healthcare in Vietnam

Healthcare in Vietnam works well for expats who plan for it. The public system is cheap but difficult to use without Vietnamese, while private and international hospitals offer a good standard of care at prices well below those in North America.

The key is to choose the right health insurance before you move, and to know which hospital you’ll use in an emergency. With those two things in place, most expats find healthcare here reliable and affordable.

Frequently Asked Questions: Healthcare in Vietnam

Do expats get access to public healthcare in Vietnam?

Yes, if you work for a Vietnamese employer on a contract of 12 months or more, you’ll usually join the national health insurance scheme. Anyone can use public hospitals by paying directly.

Is public healthcare free in Vietnam?

No. National health insurance usually pays 80% of covered costs at your registered facility, and you pay the rest. Without insurance, you pay in full, although public prices are low.

Am I legally required to have private health insurance in Vietnam?

Generally not, but it is strongly recommended. Public cover doesn’t extend to international hospitals, and treatment there can be expensive without insurance.

Do doctors in Vietnam speak English?

At international hospitals and clinics, yes. In public hospitals, many staff speak little English, so bring a Vietnamese speaker if you can.

How long does it take to see a specialist?

At private and international hospitals, you can usually book a specialist within a few days, often without a referral. In the public system, waits are longer and you need a referral for full insurance cover.

How much does a doctor’s visit cost?

A consultation at an international hospital typically costs around $60–$120. Local private hospitals and public hospitals are much cheaper.

How much does private health insurance cost?

A good regional plan often costs around $50–$120 per person per month, depending on your age, medical history and the level of cover. Worldwide plans cost more.

Can I use both public and private healthcare?

Yes. Many expats use private hospitals for most care and keep public cover through their employer as a back-up.

What are the emergency numbers in Vietnam?

Dial 115 for an ambulance, 113 for police and 114 for fire. Ambulances can be slow in city traffic, so save the emergency number of your nearest international hospital as well.

Can I bring my medication to Vietnam?

Usually yes, for personal use, in its original packaging and with your prescription. Controlled medicines are strictly limited, so check the rules before you travel.
