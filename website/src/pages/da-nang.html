Vietnam Guide: Da Nang

Living in Da Nang as an Expat

Everything you need to know about moving to Da Nang.

Da Nang is the fastest-growing expat destination in Vietnam. It has a long city beach, mountains on its doorstep, modern roads, an international airport 15 minutes from the sand and a far calmer pace than Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City, at noticeably lower cost. Digital nomads, families, retirees and professionals all find a place here. This guide covers where to live, what it costs, how to get around, work, healthcare, schools, the weather and daily life.

In July 2025, Da Nang merged with Quang Nam province, so the city now has around 3 million people and includes Hoi An. The same reform removed the district level of government, so official addresses now use wards, but everyone still uses the old names such as Son Tra and Hai Chau, and so do we.

Why Expats Choose Da Nang

  • The beach: a long stretch of sand running the length of the city, quiet by day and busy with swimmers at sunrise and sunset
  • A calmer city: big-city amenities with a laid-back feel, less traffic and less crowding than Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City
  • Cost: generally cheaper than the two big cities, especially for rent
  • Easy to get around: most places you need are within 15 to 20 minutes, and the airport is right in town
  • On the doorstep: Hoi An, Hue, the Marble Mountains, the Son Tra Peninsula, the Hai Van Pass and the Cham Islands
  • Remote work: fast internet, plenty of coworking spaces and a café on every corner

The trade-offs: it is hot and humid for much of the year, the rainy season brings storms and occasional flooding, there are fewer international schools and specialist hospitals than in the big cities, and the career market is smaller.

Best Neighborhoods for Expats in Da Nang

The Han River divides Da Nang in two. Most expats live on the east side, near the beach, while the west side is the city center and much more local. Neighborhood names can be confusing (My Khe beach and Khue My are different places), so always check the location on a map before you view an apartment.

An Thuong and My An

An Thuong, in the My An area behind My Khe beach, is the main expat and tourist hub. Its grid of streets, all named An Thuong, is packed with cafés, Western and Asian restaurants, bars, gyms, coworking spaces and grocery stores, and the beach is a short walk away. It is the easiest place to land, with everything within walking distance, but it is the most expensive area, it can feel touristy in the busy season, and you won’t get much local immersion.

Son Tra

Son Tra stretches along the northern end of the beach towards the Son Tra Peninsula. It has a more local feel, with traditional markets, residential streets and newer apartment buildings, and rents are typically 10–20% lower than in An Thuong. The beach is less developed, and in the early morning and evening it fills with locals exercising and swimming. Shops, restaurants and cafés are more spread out, so you will want a motorbike or Grab. It is popular with Korean residents and people looking for better value.

Khue My

Just south of An Thuong, between the river and the sea, Khue My is a quiet, residential area. It is a short walk or ride from An Thuong’s restaurants and cafés, with a few grocery stores and cafés of its own, and suits people who want peace without being cut off.

Ngu Hanh Son

Further south, towards the Marble Mountains and Non Nuoc beach, Ngu Hanh Son has resorts, villas, newer developments and more space. Several international schools, including the Singapore International School and APU American International School, are here, which makes it a good base for families.

Hai Chau

Hai Chau covers most of the west bank of the Han River and is Da Nang’s city center, close to the airport, the train station, Han Market and the riverside. It offers the best value for money and a real urban Vietnamese experience, but the beach is a 5 to 10 minute ride away and there are fewer Western restaurants.

Renting an Apartment in Da Nang

Da Nang has everything from studios to sea-view apartments and large villas. For a short stay of a few months, a furnished Airbnb or serviced apartment is the easiest option, and hosts will often negotiate a better monthly rate. For longer stays, most people use an agent or the local Facebook groups, such as Da Nang Apartments for Expats and Expats in Da Nang, which is one of the biggest expat groups in the country.

What rent costs

  • An Thuong and My Khe beach: around $380–$550 a month for a one-bedroom apartment
  • Son Tra: typically 10–20% less than An Thuong
  • Hai Chau: one-bedroom apartments from around $250–$350 a month
  • Sea views, new buildings and villas cost more, and luxury villas can run well over $1,000

Landlords usually ask for a deposit of one or two months’ rent, plus one to three months paid in advance, and many prefer a lease of six to twelve months. Rents are often negotiable, especially on longer leases.

Before you sign

  • View in person before paying anything
  • Ask whether the street or building floods in the rainy season, and check windows and doors for storm damage and leaks
  • Look for damp and mould, which are common in the humid months
  • Make sure the contract says who pays for utilities, internet and repairs, and how electricity is charged
  • Check that the landlord will register your temporary residence with the local police, which is required for every foreigner

Cost of Living in Da Nang

Da Nang is one of the most affordable coastal cities in Asia and generally cheaper than Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City, mainly because rent is lower. Food, transport and services are cheap, as long as you eat locally.

Typical monthly budgets

  • Single person: roughly $800–$1,200 a month for a comfortable lifestyle, including rent
  • Couple: around $2,000–$2,200 a month for a comfortable life near the beach (see our sample budget below)
  • Family of four: roughly $2,500–$4,500 a month, depending mostly on school fees

Everyday prices

  • Street food such as phở or mì Quảng: $1.50–$2.50
  • Main course at a Western restaurant: $8–$15
  • Coffee: from about $0.50 at a local café to $1–$2 for a latte
  • Local beer: from about $0.50
  • Grab ride across town: VND 40,000–100,000 ($1.50–$4)
  • Long-term motorbike rental: around $60–$80 a month
  • Coworking: around $5–$8 a day, or $70–$130 a month
  • Gym membership: around $20–$40 a month

Imported groceries, wine and Western brands cost about the same as at home, or more. Local markets are the cheapest places for fresh fruit and vegetables.

Getting Around Da Nang

Da Nang is spread out, but it is easy to get around, and traffic is much calmer than in Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City.

Ride-hailing

Grab is the easiest way to get around, with motorbike and car options. Be and Xanh SM, an all-electric service, are popular alternatives. Even people who ride their own motorbike tend to use a car in the rainy season.

Riding a motorbike

A motorbike is the most popular way to get around, and cheap to rent. Before you ride:

  • Vietnam requires a licence for any motorbike of 50cc or more. Short-stay visitors from some countries can use an International Driving Permit (1968 Convention) with their home licence, but residents need a Vietnamese licence, converted from their home licence.
  • Helmets are compulsory for the rider and passenger, so make sure your rental comes with good ones.
  • The drink-driving limit is zero.
  • Riding without the right licence can invalidate your insurance if you have an accident.

Build your confidence on quiet streets before riding on the main roads.

Buses and walking

City buses are cheap and cover the city, including routes to Hoi An, but they can be crowded, and signs are mostly in Vietnamese. Within An Thuong, My An and Khue My most things are walkable, but getting between neighborhoods, or across the river, usually means Grab or a motorbike.

Air and rail

Da Nang International Airport is in the city, about 15 minutes from the beach, with flights across Vietnam and Asia. The train station is in Hai Chau, and the train north to Hue, over the Hai Van Pass, is one of the most scenic rides in the country.

Working in Da Nang

Jobs

Da Nang’s main employers of foreigners are tourism and hospitality, English teaching in language centers and schools, and a fast-growing technology sector, with software companies and high-tech parks. Manufacturing, much of it Japanese and Korean investment, also hires foreign managers and specialists. The job market is much smaller than in Ho Chi Minh City or Hanoi, so many people arrive with remote work or their own business.

Work permits

To work for a Vietnamese employer you need a work permit, which your employer applies for. You will usually need a degree and relevant experience, a clean criminal record check and a health certificate. Work permits are valid for up to two years.

Remote work and digital nomads

Da Nang is one of Southeast Asia’s most popular bases for remote workers. The internet is fast, coworking spaces are concentrated near the beach, and there are dozens of cafés with good Wi-Fi. Remote workers still need a visa that matches how long they stay and what they do.

Healthcare and Safety in Da Nang

Hospitals and clinics

Da Nang has good private healthcare for a city its size, including Vinmec Da Nang, Family Medical Practice and Hoan My Da Nang, with English-speaking doctors. For some specialist treatment, residents travel to Ho Chi Minh City, Hanoi or Bangkok, so choose health insurance that covers evacuation. Pharmacies are everywhere, but don’t take antibiotics or strong medicines without a doctor’s advice.

Emergencies

Dial 115 for an ambulance, 113 for police and 114 for fire. Save the number of your nearest international hospital, and keep your insurance details and a copy of your passport with you.

Staying safe

Da Nang is safe, with low crime, cleaner air than the big cities and calmer roads. Watch your phone and bag in busy areas, and take care on the road, which is still the biggest everyday risk. At the beach, swim where lifeguards are on duty and stay out of the water when red flags are up. In the rainy season, follow local weather warnings, as typhoons can bring strong winds and flooding.

Tap water is not safe to drink. Use bottled or filtered water, and a refillable bottle cuts down on plastic.

International Schools in Da Nang

Da Nang has fewer international schools than Ho Chi Minh City or Hanoi, but a good range for its size:

  • Singapore International School @ Da Nang: Singapore and Cambridge curriculum, IGCSE and A-Levels
  • APU American International School: American curriculum with AP
  • Odyssey International School: an IB candidate school, between Da Nang and Hoi An
  • The St. Nicholas School: American curriculum, from kindergarten
  • Bilingual schools and preschools: including Sky-Line School, UK Academy, Little Giants and Cocoon Montessori

Published fees range from about $5,650 to $25,230 a year, depending on the school and grade. Hoi An International School, about 45 minutes south, is another option. Places in popular year groups can fill up, so apply early.

Banking, Money and Phones

Larger shops and restaurants accept cards, but cash is still needed for street food, markets and small businesses, and QR payments through banking apps, MoMo and ZaloPay are everywhere. ATMs usually limit each withdrawal, so check your bank’s fees.

A local bank account makes it easy to pay rent and use payment apps. Banks generally ask for your passport, a valid visa or residence card and proof of address. Beach Life, Food and Community

Outdoors

Life in Da Nang revolves around the sea. Swim, surf (the waves are small, ideal for beginners), paddleboard or join the locals for a sunrise dip. Ride up the Son Tra Peninsula to the giant Lady Buddha statue at Linh Ung Pagoda, look out for the rare red-shanked douc langurs, and climb the caves and shrines of the Marble Mountains. There are plenty of gyms, yoga studios and sports groups too.

In the city

The Dragon Bridge breathes fire and water on Saturday and Sunday evenings at 9pm. Visit the pink Da Nang Cathedral, the Museum of Cham Sculpture, Han Market and the night markets along the river.

Food and coffee

Try the local specialities, mì Quảng noodles and bánh xèo, and the fresh seafood along the beach. Da Nang has an excellent café scene, from tiny local spots to specialty roasters, with coconut coffee and salt coffee as local favorites.

Community

The expat community is large and friendly. Facebook groups such as Expats in Da Nang are the place for apartments, advice, meetups and events, and there are sports, surf and coworking communities too. Da Nang also has a large Korean community, and you will see many Korean shops and restaurants.

Living respectfully

Dress modestly at temples and pagodas, take your shoes off when entering someone’s home, and stay calm in disagreements, as making a scene is frowned upon. Learn a few words of Vietnamese, such as xin chào (hello) and cảm ơn (thank you), and support local businesses.

Climate in Da Nang

Da Nang is warm all year, with a dry season and a rainy season:

  • Dry season (about February to August): sunny and beach weather, getting very hot and humid from June to August, which is also the busy domestic holiday season
  • Rainy season (about September to December): heavy rain, with the risk of typhoons and flooding, especially in October and November
  • Cooler months (about December to February): mild, often cloudy and damp, but never really cold

Some people love the rainy season as a break from the heat. If you are choosing when to arrive, the dry season is the easiest time to settle in and look for an apartment.

Weekend Escapes from Da Nang

Hoi An

The lantern-lit old town of Hoi An, a UNESCO World Heritage site, is about 45 minutes south and now part of the same city. Go for the old town, the rice fields and An Bang beach, ideally on weekdays to avoid the crowds. Thinking of living there? Read our Hoi An guide.

Hue and the Hai Van Pass

The former imperial capital of Hue is a scenic train ride north. Many people ride the Hai Van Pass on the way, one of the most famous coastal roads in Vietnam.

Ba Na Hills, My Son and the Cham Islands

Ba Na Hills, with its Golden Bridge held up by giant stone hands, is a popular day out in the mountains. My Son Sanctuary has the ruins of ancient Cham temples, and the Cham Islands, off the coast from Hoi An, are good for snorkeling.

Further afield

From the airport, it is a short flight to Ho Chi Minh City, Hanoi, Da Lat and Phu Quoc, and to Seoul, Bangkok, Singapore and beyond.

Final Thoughts on Living in Da Nang

Da Nang offers something rare: a real city with a beach lifestyle, low costs, clean streets and easy access to some of Vietnam’s best sights. It suits remote workers, families and retirees who want a calmer life than in the big cities, and professionals in tourism, tech and teaching.

Get the basics right: the right visa, a work permit if you are working for a local employer, good health insurance with evacuation cover, a plan for the rainy season, and a few weeks in short-term accommodation while you choose your neighborhood.

Frequently Asked Questions: Living in Da Nang

Where do most expats live in Da Nang?

Most live on the beach side of the river, in An Thuong and My An, with others in Son Tra, Khue My and, for families, Ngu Hanh Son near the international schools. Hai Chau, the city center, is the best-value option.

How much does it cost to live in Da Nang?

A single person can live comfortably on roughly $800–$1,200 a month including rent, and a couple on around $2,000–$2,200 near the beach. Families should add school fees.

Is Da Nang cheaper than Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City?

Yes, mainly because rent is lower. A one-bedroom near the beach typically costs $380–$550 a month, compared with $700–$1,300 in Tay Ho in Hanoi and $600–$1,500 in the main expat areas of Ho Chi Minh City.

Is Da Nang good for digital nomads?

Yes. It has fast internet, affordable coworking spaces, lots of cafés to work from and a large remote-working community. You still need the right visa for your stay.

When is the rainy season in Da Nang?

From about September to December, with the highest risk of typhoons and flooding in October and November. The dry season runs from about February to August.

Is Da Nang or Hoi An better to live in?

Da Nang is a real city, with more housing, shops, hospitals and schools. Hoi An is smaller, prettier and more touristy. Many people live in Da Nang and visit Hoi An, and some live on the quieter edges of Hoi An, such as An Bang.
