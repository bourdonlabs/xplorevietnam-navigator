If you are in Vietnam on an e-visa or a visa-free stay and want to stay longer, the first question is whether you can sort it out from inside the country or need to leave. The honest answer is that it depends on your visa, your nationality and why you want to stay. This article sets out what to check, and the routes that let you live here long term without regular visa runs.

Start with what you have now

Most people arrive on one of two things:

  • An e-visa: open to citizens of every country since August 2023, valid for up to 90 days, with single or multiple entry.
  • A visa exemption: visa-free stays of up to 45 days for citizens of some countries, including the UK and several EU countries. ASEAN citizens usually get shorter visa-free stays.

Both have a fixed end date. The e-visa does not allow you to stay beyond the dates on the visa. It also does not allow you to work for a Vietnamese employer or to apply for a Temporary Residence Card (TRC).

Can you extend from inside Vietnam?

Whether your current stay can be extended without leaving depends on your visa type and nationality, and Vietnamese immigration rules change often. Fees and timelines also depend on your situation, so we do not quote a single figure here. Check your options well before your current stay ends, not in the last few days.

What is certain is that leaving it too late is the costly option. Overstaying, even by a few days, can lead to fines and problems with future applications. If you are already past your date, get advice quickly and deal with it as soon as possible rather than waiting until you leave.

Changing visa type without leaving

For many people the real goal is not a longer tourist stay but a proper long-term visa. The key points:

  • In many cases a work permit can be processed while you are in Vietnam.
  • Changing visa type from inside the country is not always possible. A tourist e-visa cannot always be changed from inside Vietnam, so check before you travel.
  • A TRC is issued on the matching visa: LĐ for work, ĐT for investors and TT for family.

If you plan to move from an e-visa to a work, investor or family visa, ask early. Choosing the wrong visa can delay your move by months.

The long-term answer: a Temporary Residence Card

If you want to stop worrying about end dates, the reliable route is a TRC linked to your work permit, investment or family tie. TRCs are usually valid for 1 to 10 years depending on the category:

  • Work (LĐ): a TRC valid for up to 2 years, matching your work permit. The TRC means no more visa runs while you hold the job.
  • Investor (ĐT1 to ĐT3): TRCs of up to 10, 5 or 3 years depending on the size of the investment. The ĐT4 tier, for investments under VND 3 billion, gives a visa of up to 12 months but no TRC.
  • Family (TT): for spouses and children of Vietnamese citizens, or of foreigners holding a work, investor or similar visa, leading to a TRC valid for up to 3 years.

Once your work permit, investment certificate or family documents are ready, a TRC usually takes a few weeks. Document preparation is normally the longest part, so get your documents legalised early: missing legalisation is one of the most common reasons applications are delayed.

What about remote workers and retirees?

Vietnam has no retirement visa and no digital nomad visa yet. Retirees usually rely on e-visas or, where eligible, a family visa. Many remote workers use the 90-day e-visa, but it is not designed for working in Vietnam, so get advice on your situation.
