A Vietnamese bank account is how you'll pay rent, receive your salary and use local payment apps. Requirements vary from bank to bank, and even from branch to branch, so it pays to have your paperwork in order before you go.

Why a local account matters

Day to day, a local account is what lets you pay rent, receive your salary and use local payment apps. Many Vietnamese banks charge little or nothing a month for a standard account.

It also matters for bigger steps later on. If you buy property in Vietnam, payments must be made through a bank account in Vietnam, in Vietnamese dong. And banks generally lend only to foreigners who live and work here, with income paid into a Vietnamese bank account.

Documents banks usually ask for

Each bank sets its own list, but you should expect to need:

  • A valid passport
  • A valid visa or Temporary Residence Card
  • Proof of your address in Vietnam (temporary residence registration)
  • A Vietnamese phone number, for SMS and banking-app verification
  • Your employment contract, if you're opening a salary account

Bring the originals with you. Some banks also ask for a minimum remaining visa validity, so check how long your visa or residence card has left before you go.

Your address comes first

Proof of address usually means your temporary residence registration. By law, your landlord or building must declare your temporary residence to the police, usually within 12 hours of your arrival. Make sure this is done, as you may need it for your bank account as well as your visa or residence card. In practice, that means organising your home before you visit a bank.

How your tax code fits in

Your personal tax code (Mã Số Thuế, or MST) is the unique identifier for your tax, financial and legal matters in Vietnam. You'll need it for personal income tax filings, employment contracts and many banking steps, including opening a Vietnamese bank account. Foreigners receive a separate 10-digit tax code.

If you work for a Vietnamese company, your HR department will typically register it for you. Freelancers and people with non-employment income can register directly at the local tax office where they live or where their income is generated. SIM cards and everyday purchases use your passport, not your tax code.

Opening an account yourself

You need to be in Vietnam, as accounts are opened at the bank. The process typically works like this:

  1. Choose a bank with branches near where you live and staff used to foreign customers.
  2. Prepare your documents from the list above.
  3. Visit a branch with the originals. Language support is not guaranteed at every branch.
  4. Set up the bank's mobile app with your Vietnamese phone number, and collect or activate your debit card.

A note for US citizens

US expats must report foreign bank accounts (FBAR) if their combined balance exceeds USD 10,000 at any point in the year, and may need to file FATCA Form 8938. Penalties for missing these can be significant. Our taxes guide covers this and other home-country obligations.
