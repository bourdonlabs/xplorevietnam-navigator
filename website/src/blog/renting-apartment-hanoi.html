Hanoi is Vietnam's capital and its cultural heart: slightly slower and more traditional than Ho Chi Minh City, and generally a little cheaper, mainly because rents are lower. For most expats, renting is the right first step. It gives you time to learn your preferred area before committing to a purchase.

Choosing a neighbourhood

Each of Hanoi's neighbourhoods has a distinct character:

  • Tay Ho (West Lake): the main expat area, around the city's largest lake, with international restaurants, cafés and a relaxed, green feel. Popular with families and long-term residents, and close to several international schools.
  • Ba Dinh: the political centre, with government buildings and embassies. Calm, green and well located for the Old Quarter and Tay Ho.
  • Hoan Kiem (Old Quarter): the historic heart, full of character and food, but crowded and noisy. Better for a first few months than for family life.
  • Cau Giay: a modern district in the west, with high-rise apartments, offices and universities. Good-value, newer apartments for professionals who work nearby.
  • Ciputra and Long Bien: Ciputra and the Vinhomes developments in Long Bien are large gated communities with villas, apartments, shops and international schools, suiting families who want space, security and a quieter life.

What rent costs

Rent is typically your largest expense. Typical monthly rents for a one-bedroom apartment are:

  • Tay Ho: $700 to $1,300
  • Cau Giay: $500 to $900

Our sample budget for a couple uses $1,200 a month for a two-bedroom apartment in Tay Ho. Rents are lower outside Tay Ho and the Old Quarter. Prices in Tay Ho have risen in recent years, and good apartments at fair prices move quickly. Rents are negotiable, especially on longer leases, and many buildings charge a monthly management fee.

Leases, deposits and paperwork

Most leases run 6 to 12 months, and shorter leases usually cost more per month. Contracts are often in Vietnamese and English. Landlords typically ask for a security deposit of one to two months' rent, plus one to three months of rent in advance. Agent fees for rentals are usually paid by the landlord.

To rent, you will generally need:

  • A valid passport
  • A valid visa or temporary residence card
  • The deposit and first rent payment

By law, your landlord or building must declare your temporary residence to the police, usually within 12 hours of your arrival. Make sure this is done, as you may need it for your visa, residence card or bank account.

Note that renting a home in Vietnam for 183 days or more in a tax year can make you a Vietnamese tax resident, unless you can show you are tax resident in another country. See our taxes guide.

Utilities

Water and internet are cheap. Electricity is the one to watch in Hanoi's hot, humid summers: expect $40 to $60 a month in cooler months, and $60 to $100 or more in the hot season with regular air conditioning. Many landlords charge a flat rate for electricity of around VND 3,500 to 4,000 per kWh, higher than the official EVN tariff, so check your lease.

Where to find rentals

There is no central listing system in Vietnam. The main websites are batdongsan.com.vn and Nhà Tốt (chotot.com), and many rentals are found through agents and local Facebook groups. Online photos and prices are not always accurate, so view a property, in person or by video, before paying.

Searching from abroad is common, and most agents are used to organising video walkthroughs.
